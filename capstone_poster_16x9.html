Introduction

Thirty-five percent of children with autism have not achieved consistent toileting by age three. For many, the challenge follows them into adulthood, and it falls on their caregivers to manage it every single day. The tools available to these families have not changed in decades: fixed schedules, rigid prompting, constant vigilance.

This study asked a simple question: can we do better?

Research Questions

RQ1 Can ML predict when a toileting event is likely, using data a caregiver could realistically collect?
RQ2 Do wearable physiological signals (HR, HRV) add predictive value beyond caregiver-logged features?
RQ3 Can model outputs be translated into clinically actionable caregiver alerts?

Conclusions

1
Personalized toileting prediction is feasible using 5 years of caregiver-logged data — AUPRC gains of 36–116% over baseline.
2
Wearable tech is not required. Schedule, intake, and void history alone achieve best performance.
3
Top 4 features are all caregiver-trackable, enabling deployment in resource-limited settings.
4
At the 60-min horizon, the model captures 72% of urination events, outperforming fixed-schedule prompting.
Future Work: Multi-participant replication · Reinforcement learning for alert optimization · Mobile app development
