Introduction

Thirty-five percent of children with autism have not achieved consistent toileting by age three. For many, the challenge follows them into adulthood, and it falls on their caregivers to manage it every single day. The tools available to these families have not changed in decades: fixed schedules, rigid prompting, constant vigilance. This study asked a simple question: can we do better?

Conclusions

1
Personalized toileting prediction is feasible using five years of caregiver-logged data, with AUPRC gains of 36% to 116% over baseline.
2
Wearable technology is not required. Schedule, intake, and void history alone achieve the best performance.
3
The top four predictive features are all trackable by a caregiver or simple mobile app, making deployment accessible in resource-limited settings.
4
At the 60-minute horizon, the model captures 72% of urination events, meaningfully improving upon fixed-schedule prompting.
